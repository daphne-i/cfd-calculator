Knudsen Number (Kn)

The Knudsen number (Kn) is the ratio of the molecular mean free path to a physical length scale. It determines whether a gas can be modeled as a continuous fluid or as a collection of individual molecules (rarefied gas).

0.00

Flow classification based on Knudsen number (Kn):

  • Continuum Flow: Kn < 0.01
  • Slip Flow: 0.01 ≤ Kn < 0.1
  • Transitional Flow: 0.1 ≤ Kn < 10
  • Free Molecular Flow: Kn > 10

Prandtl Number (Pr)

The Prandtl number (Pr) is the ratio of momentum diffusivity to thermal diffusivity. It compares the thickness of the momentum and thermal boundary layers in a fluid.

0.00

Typical Prandtl number (Pr) values for common fluids:

  • Liquid Metals (Mercury, Sodium): Pr << 1 (e.g., ~0.02)
  • Gases (Air, Helium): Pr ≈ 0.7
  • Water (@ 20°C): Pr ≈ 7
  • Engine Oils: Pr >> 1 (e.g., 100 - 40,000)

Nusselt Number (Nu)

The Nusselt number (Nu) represents the ratio of convective to conductive heat transfer across a boundary. A higher value indicates more effective convection.

0.00

Interpreting the Nusselt number (Nu):

  • Nu = 1: Heat transfer is by pure conduction (stagnant fluid).
  • 1 < Nu < 100: Heat transfer is dominated by laminar flow convection.
  • Nu > 100: Heat transfer is dominated by turbulent flow convection.

Weber Number (We)

The Weber number (We) compares a fluid's inertia to its surface tension. It's crucial in multiphase flows with interfaces, such as analyzing droplets and bubbles.

0.00

Interpreting the Weber number (We):

  • We << 1: Surface tension is dominant (e.g., droplets remain spherical).
  • We >> 1: Inertial forces are dominant (e.g., sprays, droplet breakup).
  • For a falling liquid drop, breakup typically begins when We > 10.
